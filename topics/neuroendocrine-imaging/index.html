Neuroendocrine Imaging: ACR Appropriateness Criteria summary

Which imaging to order

9 clinical scenarios from the ACR Appropriateness Criteria topic "Neuroendocrine Imaging", grouped by the imaging study ACR rates highest for each. Studies listed together share the same ACR category; ACR does not rank studies within a category.

Independent summary of the ACR Appropriateness Criteria as of November 2025. Not affiliated with or endorsed by the American College of Radiology. For education; confirm against the linked source and your institution's protocols.

Hypopituitarism known

Adult. Suspected or known hypofunctioning pituitary gland (hypopituitarism, growth hormone deficiency, growth deceleration, panhypopituitarism, hypogonadotropic hypogonadism). Initial imaging.

May be appropriate: MRI sella with IV contrast; CT sella with IV contrast

ACR source for this scenario

Hypopituitarism suspected

Adult. Suspected or known hypofunctioning pituitary gland (hypopituitarism, growth hormone deficiency, growth deceleration, panhypopituitarism, hypogonadotropic hypogonadism). Initial imaging.

May be appropriate: MRI sella with IV contrast; CT sella with IV contrast

ACR source for this scenario

Pituitary adenoma

Adult. Suspected or known hyperfunctioning pituitary adenoma (hyperthyroidism [high thyroid-stimulating hormone], Cushing syndrome [high adrenal corticotrophic hormone], hyperprolactinemia, acromegaly, or gigantism). Initial imaging.

May be appropriate: MRI sella with IV contrast; CT sella with IV contrast; CT sella without IV contrast
