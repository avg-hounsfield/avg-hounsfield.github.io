Radiologic Management of Mesenteric Ischemia: ACR Appropriateness Criteria summary

Which imaging to order

7 clinical scenarios from the ACR Appropriateness Criteria topic "Radiologic Management of Mesenteric Ischemia", grouped by the imaging study ACR rates highest for each. Studies listed together share the same ACR category; ACR does not rank studies within a category.

Independent summary of the ACR Appropriateness Criteria as of November 2025. Not affiliated with or endorsed by the American College of Radiology. For education; confirm against the linked source and your institution's protocols.

Usually Appropriate Surgery with median arcuate ligament release; Mesenteric angiography in lateral projection during both inspiration and expiration

1 scenario

Abdominal pain after meals and CTA showing widely patent origins of SMA and IMA, with compression of the celiac origin by the median arcuate ligament. Initial therapy.

Abdominal pain after meals and CTA showing widely patent origins of SMA and IMA, with compression of the celiac origin by the median arcuate ligament. Initial therapy.

May be appropriate: Supportive measures only; Percutaneous transluminal angioplasty with stent placement

Usually Appropriate Angiography with possible percutaneous transluminal angioplasty and stent placement

1 scenario

History of abdominal pain after meals for the past few months and weight loss. CTA shows aortic atherosclerotic disease and suggests SMA-origin stenosis with occlusion of celiac origin and an occluded IMA. Initial therapy.

History of abdominal pain after meals for the past few months and weight loss. CTA shows aortic atherosclerotic disease and suggests SMA-origin stenosis with occlusion of celiac origin and an occluded IMA. Initial therapy.

May be appropriate: Surgical bypass or endarterectomy; Systemic anticoagulation

Usually Appropriate Angiography with infusion of vasodilator; Systemic anticoagulation

1 scenario

Patient with cardiac disease causing low cardiac output who developed abdominal pain but without peritoneal signs. CTA shows patent origins and proximal portions of celiac artery, SMA, and IMA, with diffuse irregular narrowing of SMA branches. Initial therapy.

Patient with cardiac disease causing low cardiac output who developed abdominal pain but without peritoneal signs. CTA shows patent origins and proximal portions of celiac artery, SMA, and IMA, with diffuse irregular narrowing of SMA branches. Initial therapy.

May be appropriate: Systemic infusion of prostaglandin E1

Usually Appropriate Systemic anticoagulation; Transhepatic superior mesenteric vein catheterization and pharmacomechanical thrombolysis

1 scenario

Previously healthy with worsening diffuse abdominal pain for 2 weeks. CTA shows occlusion of the superior mesenteric vein and its major tributaries. Bowel appears normal. Serum lactate level is normal. Initial therapy.

Previously healthy with worsening diffuse abdominal pain for 2 weeks. CTA shows occlusion of the superior mesenteric vein and its major tributaries. Bowel appears normal. Serum lactate level is normal. Initial therapy.

May be appropriate: Transjugular superior mesenteric vein catheterization and pharmacomechanical thrombolysis and TIPS; SMA angiography followed by thrombolytic infusion
