Acute Trauma to the Foot: ACR Appropriateness Criteria summary

Which imaging to order

9 clinical scenarios from the ACR Appropriateness Criteria topic "Acute Trauma to the Foot", grouped by the imaging study ACR rates highest for each. Studies listed together share the same ACR category; ACR does not rank studies within a category.

Independent summary of the ACR Appropriateness Criteria as of November 2025. Not affiliated with or endorsed by the American College of Radiology. For education; confirm against the linked source and your institution's protocols.

Foot trauma, dislocation suspected, radiography nondiagnostic, next imaging study

Adult or child older than 5 years of age. Acute trauma to the foot. Suspect Lisfranc injury, tendon injury, or occult fracture or dislocation. Radiographs are normal or equivocal. Next imaging study.

May be appropriate: US foot

Usually Appropriate Radiography foot; Radiography foot with weightbearing

2 scenarios

Foot trauma, Ottawa rules negative, injury suspected in area not addressed by Ottawa, initial imaging

Adult or child older than 5 years of age. Acute trauma to the foot. Ottawa rules can be evaluated without exclusionary criteria. Ottawa rules are negative. Suspected pathology in an anatomic area not addressed by Ottawa rules (not involving the midfoot; eg, metatarsal-phalangeal joint, metatarsal, toe, tendon, etc). Initial imaging.

May be appropriate: CT foot without IV contrast
