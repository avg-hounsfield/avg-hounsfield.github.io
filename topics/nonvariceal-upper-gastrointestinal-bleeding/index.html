Nonvariceal Upper Gastrointestinal Bleeding: which imaging to order

7 clinical scenarios from the ACR Appropriateness Criteria topic "Nonvariceal Upper Gastrointestinal Bleeding", grouped by the imaging study ACR rates highest for each. Studies listed together share the same ACR category; ACR does not rank studies within a category.

Independent summary of the ACR Appropriateness Criteria as of November 2025. Not affiliated with or endorsed by the American College of Radiology. For education; confirm against the linked source and your institution's protocols.

Usually Appropriate Arteriography visceral; CTA abdomen and pelvis without and with IV contrast

4 scenarios

Upper GI bleeding, nonvariceal, clear source on endoscopy, continued bleeding after treatment, initial imaging

Adult. Endoscopy confirms nonvariceal upper gastrointestinal bleeding with a clear source, but treatment not possible or continued bleeding after endoscopic treatment. Initial imaging.

May be appropriate: CTA chest without and with IV contrast; CTA abdomen without and with IV contrast

ACR source for this scenario

Upper GI bleeding, nonvariceal, postsurgical cause, endoscopy contraindicated, initial imaging

Adult. Postsurgical or traumatic causes of nonvariceal upper gastrointestinal bleeding. Endoscopy is contraindicated. Initial imaging.

May be appropriate: CT abdomen and pelvis with IV contrast; CTA chest without and with IV contrast; CT abdomen and pelvis without and with IV contrast; CT enterography

ACR source for this scenario

Upper GI bleeding, nonvariceal, no clear source on endoscopy, initial imaging

Adult. Endoscopy confirms nonvariceal upper gastrointestinal bleeding without a clear source. Initial imaging.

May be appropriate: Arteriography visceral; CTA chest without and with IV contrast; RBC scan abdomen and pelvis; CT enterography
