Suspected Spine Infection: ACR Appropriateness Criteria summary

Which imaging to order

97 clinical scenarios from the ACR Appropriateness Criteria topic "Suspected Spine Infection", grouped by the imaging study ACR rates highest for each. Studies listed together share the same ACR category; ACR does not rank studies within a category.

Independent summary of the ACR Appropriateness Criteria as of November 2025. Not affiliated with or endorsed by the American College of Radiology. For education; confirm against the linked source and your institution's protocols.

Usually Appropriate MRI lumbar spine without and with IV contrast; MRI lumbar spine without IV contrast

14 scenarios

Spine infection suspected, lumbar, CT abnormal, next imaging study

Suspected spine infection (such as epidural abscess or discitis osteomyelitis). Abnormal radiographs or CT findings. Next imaging study.

May be appropriate: 3-phase bone scan complete spine; FDG-PET/CT whole body; Gallium scan whole body

ACR source for this scenario

Spine infection suspected, lumbar, cauda equina syndrome, initial imaging

Suspected spine infection (such as epidural abscess or discitis osteomyelitis), with new neurologic deficit or cauda equina syndrome. Initial imaging.

ACR source for this scenario

ACR source for this scenario
