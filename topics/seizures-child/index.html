Seizures-Child: ACR Appropriateness Criteria summary

Which imaging to order

8 clinical scenarios from the ACR Appropriateness Criteria topic "Seizures-Child", grouped by the imaging study ACR rates highest for each. Studies listed together share the same ACR category; ACR does not rank studies within a category.

Independent summary of the ACR Appropriateness Criteria as of November 2025. Not affiliated with or endorsed by the American College of Radiology. For education; confirm against the linked source and your institution's protocols.

Usually Appropriate MRI head without IV contrast

4 scenarios

Seizure, focal, not abuse, initial imaging Pediatric

Children 1 month to 17 years of age. Focal seizures, not including abusive head trauma. Initial imaging.

May be appropriate: MRI head without and with IV contrast; CT head without IV contrast

ACR source for this scenario

Seizure, generalized, neuro deficit, initial imaging Pediatric

Children 1 month to 17 years of age. Generalized seizure (neurologically abnormal). Initial imaging.

May be appropriate: MRI head without and with IV contrast; CT head without IV contrast

ACR source for this scenario

Seizure, neonate, initial imaging Pediatric

Neonatal seizures, age 0 to 29 days. Initial imaging.

May be appropriate: US head; MRI head without and with IV contrast; CT head without IV contrast

ACR source for this scenario

Seizure, refractory Pediatric

Children 1 month to 17 years of age. Intractable seizures or refractory epilepsy.

May be appropriate: MRI head without and with IV contrast; FDG-PET/CT brain; SPECT or SPECT/CT brain perfusion

ACR source for this scenario
