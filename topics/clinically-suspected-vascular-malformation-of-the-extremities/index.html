Clinically Suspected Vascular Malformation of the Extremities: ACR Appropriateness Criteria summary

Which imaging to order

24 clinical scenarios from the ACR Appropriateness Criteria topic "Clinically Suspected Vascular Malformation of the Extremities", grouped by the imaging study ACR rates highest for each. Studies listed together share the same ACR category; ACR does not rank studies within a category.

Independent summary of the ACR Appropriateness Criteria as of November 2025. Not affiliated with or endorsed by the American College of Radiology. For education; confirm against the linked source and your institution's protocols.

Usually Appropriate US duplex Doppler ankle; MRA ankle without and with IV contrast; MRI ankle without and with IV contrast; CTA ankle with IV contrast

2 scenarios

Vascular malformation suspected, ankle

Upper or lower extremity. Suspected vascular malformation presenting with pain or findings of physical deformity including soft-tissue mass, diffuse or focal enlargement, discoloration, or ulceration. Initial imaging.

May be appropriate: US ankle with IV contrast; MRA ankle without IV contrast; MRI ankle without IV contrast; CT ankle with IV contrast; CT ankle without IV contrast

ACR source for this scenario

Vascular murmur, ankle

Upper or lower extremity. Vascular murmur (bruit or thrill). Initial imaging.

May be appropriate: US ankle with IV contrast; Arteriography ankle; MRA ankle without IV contrast; MRI ankle without IV contrast; CT ankle with IV contrast; CT ankle without and with IV contrast

ACR source for this scenario
