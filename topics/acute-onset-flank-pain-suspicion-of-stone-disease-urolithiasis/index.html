Acute Onset Flank Pain-Suspicion of Stone Disease (Urolithiasis): ACR Appropriateness Criteria summary

Which imaging to order

6 clinical scenarios from the ACR Appropriateness Criteria topic "Acute Onset Flank Pain-Suspicion of Stone Disease (Urolithiasis)", grouped by the imaging study ACR rates highest for each. Studies listed together share the same ACR category; ACR does not rank studies within a category.

Independent summary of the ACR Appropriateness Criteria as of November 2025. Not affiliated with or endorsed by the American College of Radiology. For education; confirm against the linked source and your institution's protocols.

Usually Appropriate CT abdomen and pelvis without IV contrast

3 scenarios

Flank pain, acute, stone disease on recent imaging, recurrent symptoms, follow-up imaging

Acute onset flank pain in patient with known current stone disease, diagnosed on recent imaging. Recurrent symptoms of stone disease. Follow-up imaging.

May be appropriate: US color Doppler kidneys and bladder retroperitoneal; US kidneys and bladder retroperitoneal; Radiography abdomen and pelvis; CT abdomen and pelvis with IV contrast

ACR source for this scenario

Flank pain, acute, stone disease suspected, no hx of stone disease, initial imaging

Acute onset flank pain. Suspicion of stone disease. No history or remote history of stone disease. Initial imaging.

May be appropriate: US color Doppler kidneys and bladder retroperitoneal; US kidneys and bladder retroperitoneal; Radiography abdomen and pelvis

ACR source for this scenario

Flank pain, acute, stone disease suspected, remote hx of stone disease, initial imaging

Acute onset flank pain. Suspicion of stone disease. No history or remote history of stone disease. Initial imaging.

May be appropriate: US color Doppler kidneys and bladder retroperitoneal; US kidneys and bladder retroperitoneal; Radiography abdomen and pelvis

ACR source for this scenario

Usually Appropriate US kidneys and bladder retroperitoneal

2 scenarios

Flank pain, acute, stone disease suspected, pregnant, follow-up imaging

Pregnant patient. Acute onset flank pain. Suspicion of stone disease. Initial or follow-up imaging.

May be appropriate: US color Doppler kidneys and bladder retroperitoneal; MRU without IV contrast; CT abdomen and pelvis without IV contrast

ACR source for this scenario
