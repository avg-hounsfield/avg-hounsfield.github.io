Chronic Knee Pain: which imaging to order

6 clinical scenarios from the ACR Appropriateness Criteria topic "Chronic Knee Pain", grouped by the imaging study ACR rates highest for each. Studies listed together share the same ACR category; ACR does not rank studies within a category.

Independent summary of the ACR Appropriateness Criteria as of November 2025. Not affiliated with or endorsed by the American College of Radiology. For education; confirm against the linked source and your institution's protocols.

Usually Appropriate MRI knee without IV contrast

4 scenarios

Knee pain, chronic, joint effusion on radiography

Adult or child greater than or equal to 5 years of age. Chronic knee pain. Initial knee radiograph negative or demonstrates joint effusion. Next imaging procedure.

May be appropriate: US knee; Radiography hip ipsilateral; Radiography lumbar spine; Image-guided aspiration knee; MR arthrography knee; CT arthrography knee; CT knee without IV contrast

ACR source for this scenario

Knee pain, chronic, negative radiography

Adult or child greater than or equal to 5 years of age. Chronic knee pain. Initial knee radiograph negative or demonstrates joint effusion. Next imaging procedure.

May be appropriate: US knee; Radiography hip ipsilateral; Radiography lumbar spine; Image-guided aspiration knee; MR arthrography knee; CT arthrography knee; CT knee without IV contrast

ACR source for this scenario

Knee pain, chronic, osteochondritis or loose bodies or prev surgery on radiography

Adult or child greater than or equal to 5 years of age. Chronic knee pain. Initial knee radiograph demonstrates osteochondritis dissecans (OCD), loose bodies, or history of cartilage or meniscal repair. Next imaging procedure.

May be appropriate: MR arthrography knee; CT arthrography knee; CT knee without IV contrast

ACR source for this scenario

Knee pain, chronic, prior injury on radiography

Adult or child greater than or equal to 5 years of age. Chronic knee pain. Initial knee radiograph demonstrates signs of prior osseous injury (ie, Segond fracture, tibial spine avulsion, etc.). Next imaging procedure.

May be appropriate: CT arthrography knee; CT knee without IV contrast

ACR source for this scenario
