Acute Trauma to the Knee: ACR Appropriateness Criteria summary

Which imaging to order

11 clinical scenarios from the ACR Appropriateness Criteria topic "Acute Trauma to the Knee", grouped by the imaging study ACR rates highest for each. Studies listed together share the same ACR category; ACR does not rank studies within a category.

Independent summary of the ACR Appropriateness Criteria as of November 2025. Not affiliated with or endorsed by the American College of Radiology. For education; confirm against the linked source and your institution's protocols.

Usually Appropriate Radiography knee

4 scenarios

Knee trauma, fall or twisting, cannot bear weight, initial imaging

Adult or child 5 years of age or older. Fall or acute twisting trauma to the knee. One or more of the following: focal tenderness, effusion, inability to bear weight. Initial imaging.

ACR source for this scenario

Knee trauma, fall or twisting, internal derangement suspected, no fracture on radiography, next imaging study Pediatric

Adult or skeletally mature child. Fall or acute twisting trauma to the knee. No fracture seen on radiographs. Suspect occult fracture or internal derangement. Next study.

May be appropriate: CT knee without IV contrast

Adult or child 5 years of age or older. Significant trauma to the knee (eg, motor vehicle accident, knee dislocation). Initial imaging.
