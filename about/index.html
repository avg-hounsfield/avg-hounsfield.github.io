About GRAi Imaging Guide

Formerly called Radex.

GRAi Imaging Guide is an educational reference that helps clinicians find generic imaging guidance quickly, without working through the full ACR Appropriateness Criteria scenario by scenario. It is an independent project and is not affiliated with or endorsed by the American College of Radiology.

Built by

Patrick Matulich, DO
Radiology Resident | GRAi

GRAi Imaging Guide was developed as a capstone project in radiology AI, motivated by a recurring challenge in clinical training: imaging appropriateness guidelines exist, but accessing them quickly at the point of care remains cumbersome. GRAi Imaging Guide is designed to reduce time-to-answer from minutes to seconds while keeping every answer traceable to its source.

Key statistics

  • 3,900+ clinical scenarios across 260+ topics
  • 72+ MRI protocols with sequence-level guidance
  • Every recommendation links to its ACR source scenario
  • Client-side AI inference - fully private, no data sent to servers
  • Offline-capable via Progressive Web App (PWA)

Data sources and methodology

Source: Guidance is summarized from the ACR Appropriateness Criteria, evidence-based guidelines developed by the American College of Radiology. GRAi Imaging Guide shows when its data was captured, and that date may lag ACR updates, so confirm against the linked source.

How answers are built: A broad search opens a topic guide that groups scenarios by what they recommend. A specific search goes to matching scenarios, with pregnancy and patient age applied as filters. ACR categories are shown as published, including panel disagreement flags, and studies in the same category are listed together because ACR does not rank them.

MRI Protocols: Sample protocols based on common clinical practice. Your institution may have different sequences, parameters, or protocols based on scanner hardware, software versions, radiologist preferences, and institutional policies.

AI: When no scenario matches a search directly, a small model running in your browser suggests the closest scenarios by meaning. These results are labelled as closest matches. Nothing you type is sent to a server for search.

Important notice

This tool is provided for educational purposes only. It is not a substitute for clinical judgment, institutional protocols, or direct consultation with radiologists. Imaging decisions should always be made in the context of individual patient circumstances.
